## What to change

- **Opening:** State the reader's problem and what the guide delivers in the first paragraph. No throat-clearing or "in today's landscape."
- **Tone:** Founder-practitioner, direct, opinionated where fair. Use "we" for product and research context, but keep sentences connected (avoid thread-style fragments and one-line zingers).
- **Specificity:** Name tools, integration patterns, and tradeoffs plainly. Replace vague "better AI" or "robust platform" phrasing with concrete fit guidance.
- **Buyer sections:** In **Why you need [X]**, **Evaluation criteria**, and **Ask these questions in any demo**, interpret and sharpen — don't only list bullets.
- **Closings:** End sections and the piece on concrete observations, not taglines or triple-hammer pitch lines.

## Buyer's-guide guardrails

This genre is **procurement and architecture copy**, not a hot take thread. Apply founder voice with:

- Calm confidence — no competitor names, trash talk, or un sourced claims (same as polish)
- Observational over prescriptive ("teams that need X often hit Y") instead of lecturing
- No credibility flexes ("thousands of deployments") unless already verified in research artifacts
- Blog-appropriate register: **no emoji bullets**, no thread numbering, no LinkedIn-style "first line hook" gimmicks that fight the outline
- Preserve **Ask these questions in any demo** as a distinct H2 with numbered demo questions
