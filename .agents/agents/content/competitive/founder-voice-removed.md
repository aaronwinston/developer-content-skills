## What to change in this step

In the opening, state the reader’s problem and what the guide will help them decide. Avoid throat-clearing openers such as “in today’s landscape.”

Write with direct, fair opinions where you can support them, and use “we” only when it accurately describes product or research context. Keep sentences connected rather than chaining short fragments.

Name tools, integration patterns, and tradeoffs plainly, and explain your conclusions in the evaluation framing and in each alternative’s bottom line rather than listing features without interpretation.

Stay within comparison copy rather than thread cadence. Keep calm confidence, prefer observational framing over lecturing, do not add unverified credibility claims unless they already appear in the research artifacts, and avoid emoji bullets or hook lines that fight the outline.
