# Channel strategy (example)

> Example channel strategy from a developer-tools social program. Treat handles, cadence, and mix as a template — replace with your brand account and calendar.

Excerpt from a 2026 social strategy — **Channel strategy** tab. Use this to
shape **what kind of corporate post** to write on each channel (role, content
mix, cadence intent). This skill still drafts **@arizeai / brand page copy
only**; founder and employee advocacy stay out of scope unless the user
explicitly redirects.

**Priority order:** X → LinkedIn → YouTube.

## 1. X (the center of gravity)

Where AI engineers are and where the conversation happens in real time. Most
important channel; people-first model matters most here.

### Role

Technical takes, build-in-public, real-time reaction to the news cycle (model
releases, papers, competitor moves), threads spun out of deep-dive content, and
celebration of the community and customers. **Less announcing, more conversing.**

### Brand handle vs people

The engine is **people first, brand second**. Volume and credibility come from
founders, DevRel, and maintainers. The **@arizeai** handle:

- Amplifies people-first voices
- Runs brand-level posts (launches, topical fun, milestone threads)
- Does daily engagement (reply and quote-tweet into conversations buyers are already having)

When drafting for @arizeai with this skill: prefer brand-level amplification,
launch/milestone/community posts, and teachable technical takes — not ghosting
a founder voice onto the brand account.

### Cadence and behavior (inform drafts)

- Brand handle: roughly **one substantive post a day**, plus active daily engagement
- **Reply and quote-tweet more than you broadcast** (for draft-only work: prefer
  conversational / additive angles over pure broadcast promo when the source allows)
- Thread every deep-dive blog and every interview into a **native X thread**; the
  thread is often read more than the article
- React to the news cycle **within hours, not days** when the source is timely
- Never open with "Excited to announce." Lead with the claim, the number, or the tension

### Drafting implications for this skill

| Source type | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| Deep-dive blog / interview | Native X thread that teaches; link late |
| Launch / milestone | Brand-level post; still lead with claim or number, not announce-speak |
| News-cycle / model release | Sharp timely take within hours; conversational |
| Community / customer / guest win | Celebrate others; brand least visible |

## 2. LinkedIn (the buyer, the reach, and recruiting)

### Role

Enterprise stakeholders, AI product managers, engineering leaders, and founders.
Executive narrative, company milestones, recruiting and culture, and longer-form
versions of the category story. PR/AR enterprise-buyer audience lives here.

### Brand page vs people

Lead with executives and employees, not the brand page — but **this skill drafts
brand-page copy**. Keep brand drafts secondary in energy: clearer "what this
means and why it matters," less code-dense than X, still human (Warm-to-Sharp).

### Cadence and content (inform drafts)

- Brand page: **three to four times a week** (quality over filler)
- Content mix: category narratives, customer stories, milestone moments
  (e.g. Phoenix scale milestones, funding, launches), annual report moments,
  recruiting and culture
- **Less code than X**, more meaning and stakes for buyers/leaders

### Drafting implications for this skill

| Source type | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| Blog / technical deep dive | Longer "why this matters" cut; one concrete example, less raw code |
| Launch / milestone | Category or outcome framing; still no Excited-to announce |
| Event / webinar / Observe | Enterprise-buyer hook + clear CTA |
| Recruiting / culture | Specific and credible — not generic employer-brand fluff |
| Customer story | Outcome + specificity; flag ping-the-team if naming the customer |

## 3. YouTube (context only — not a draft target for this skill)

Evergreen education library: tutorials, frontier interviews, technical deep
dives, Observe sessions, demos. Feeds X and LinkedIn via clips and writeups.

When a source is a YouTube video or session, draft **X/LinkedIn companions**
(clip tease, one lesson, interview spotlight) — do not draft YouTube scripts
unless the user asks for a different skill/path.
