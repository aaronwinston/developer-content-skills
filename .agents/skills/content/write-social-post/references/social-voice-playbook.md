# Arize Social Voice Playbook

Source: Arize Social Voice Playbook (internal). Use this as the primary voice
and tone context when drafting corporate social copy. It builds on the broader
messaging guide and prioritizes how strong developer and tech voices actually
post.

**Core rule:** be useful, be specific, be a person. The logo is the smallest
thing in the post. The brand is what people feel after watching you post for a
year, not what the logo looks like.

**One rule the whole playbook protects:** A rough, specific, true post from a
practitioner beats a polished brand post every time.

## What the best accounts actually do

Patterns to steal (not copy):

| Example | Lesson |
|---|---|
| TBPN | Feel over facts. Unseriously serious. Deliberate narrowness creates signal density. Humor opens the door to specific, lower-status takes. Discipline beats inspiration — show up daily. |
| Cursor | Show the work. Launches are shown, not announced. A short clip of the thing working beats a paragraph about it. Be a participant in a scene, not a billboard. |
| Founder accounts (e.g. Guillermo Rauch) | Person first. A real person with a clear position outperforms a brand account. Restraint is also a voice — terse, confident, product-forward. |

For **corporate @arizeai** drafts, aim for the Cursor / practitioner end of the
spectrum: show the work, stay specific, keep personality without pretending the
brand handle is a founder account.

## The Arize voice, in five principles

1. **Teach, don't announce** — The most valuable post barely mentions Arize.
   Show a pattern, a failure and its fix, a number, a tool. If the reader learns
   something whether or not they ever buy, you did it right.
2. **Sound like a person** — Write the way you'd explain it to a smart colleague
   at the next desk. First person, opinions allowed, no corporate hedging. If it
   reads like it was approved by a committee, rewrite it.
3. **Be specific to the point of narrow** — Signal density comes from refusing
   to generalize. "Cline +15% on SWE-Bench with this ruleset" beats "improving
   agent performance." Name the model, the number, the exact failure. The
   audience is technical; talk to them like it.
4. **React fast, in voice** — A sharp take on today's model release is worth
   more than a planned post next week. Reply, quote-tweet, and add something
   useful within hours. Engagement beats broadcast.
5. **Celebrate other people** — The most credible thing we publish is someone
   else's work. Spotlight a Phoenix contributor, quote a maintainer, root for a
   guest. Developer love is earned when the brand is least visible as a brand.

## The safe-to-spicy spectrum

Every post lives somewhere from safe to spicy. All four zones are legitimate —
pick per post, per channel, and per comfort. Nobody has to live at the spicy
end. The goal is to move the company's center of gravity off "safe" as the only
setting.

| Zone | What it sounds like | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Safe** | Milestones, launches, "here's what shipped." Factual, on-message. | Brand page, company news, LinkedIn |
| **Warm** | A person adding their own take to company news. "Why this matters to me." | Employee advocacy, exec posts |
| **Sharp** | A clear opinion on the field, a real experiment with numbers, a teardown. | Founders, DevRel, technical staff on X |
| **Spicy** | News-cycle reaction, dry topical humor, a contrarian-but-true position. | Confident individual voices, in-scene |

### Channel defaults

| Channel | Aim for |
|---|---|
| **X** | Sharp-to-Spicy. This is the scene; safe posts disappear. |
| **LinkedIn** | Warm-to-Sharp. Buyers and leaders, but still human. |
| **YouTube** | Safe-to-Warm in framing; depth carries the interest. |
| **Brand handle (@arizeai)** | Up to Spicy on topical fun; **Safe** on anything that touches customers or legal. |

## Do and don't

### Green light (do)

- Lead with the claim, the number, or the tension.
- Post the rough thing. Consistency beats polish for this audience.
- Turn a Slack answer, a debugging win, or a broken build into a post.
- Reply and quote-tweet more than you broadcast.
- Use dry, specific humor. Screenshots, real logs, real numbers.
- Credit and tag the people whose work you're building on.

### Red light (don't)

- Never open with "Excited to announce."
- Don't post about the product when you could teach a pattern instead.
- Don't sand the personality off a founder's draft to make it "on brand."
- Don't chase a broad audience — narrow and specific wins.
- Don't punch down, dunk on customers, or subtweet competitors by name.
- Don't let a hot take about a customer or a claim go out without a gut-check
  (see guardrails below).

## Before you hit post

### 10-second checklist

- Would a developer learn something even if they never buy?
- Is it specific — a real number, model, or failure?
- Does it sound like a person, not like marketing?
- Did I lead with the point, not "Excited to…"?

### Guardrails (ping the team — second set of eyes required)

Speed is the default; these four are the exceptions. Everything else, just post
(after human review for corporate drafts produced by this skill).

1. Naming or characterizing a **specific customer**
2. **Unreleased** features, roadmap, or numbers not yet public
3. Anything that **names a competitor critically**
4. A take you're **not sure is true** — accuracy outranks speed

When any of these apply, flag them clearly in the skill output under **Notes for
the human** and do not present the draft as ready to ship.
