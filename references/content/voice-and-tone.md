# Voice and tone

> Example voice guide from a developer-tools company. Swap product names, proof points, and audience labels for yours. The five principles travel well to any practitioner-to-practitioner program.

How we say it. These are the rules of the room.

## Voice principles

Five principles that define what Arize sounds like. If a sentence violates one of these, rewrite it.

1. Practitioner, not pundit
We write like people who have shipped the thing. We say "we tried this and it didn't work, so we tried that," not "organizations are exploring approaches." The voice of someone debugging at 2am, not someone giving a keynote.

2. Calm authority, not hype
We do not use superlatives we cannot prove. We do not say "revolutionary" or "game-changing" or "unlock the power of." We describe what the thing does and trust the reader to draw their own conclusion. Outcomes over adjectives, always.

3. Teach, do not sell
If a paragraph could be lifted out of our content and dropped into a good technical blog without anyone noticing it was marketing, we are doing it right. We teach because we know, and the product sells itself when the teaching is good.

4. Show specifics, name names
We name the tool, the status, the function, the filename. "Four task statuses: pending, in_progress, completed, blocked." Not "a variety of task states." Specificity is credibility.

5. Talk to equals
Our reader is at least as smart as us and often smarter in their domain. We assume intelligence. We do not over-explain the basics or soften the technical parts. We trust them to keep up, and in return they trust us to be worth their time.

## Tone across contexts

| Context | Tone | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering blog post | Peer-to-peer, storytelling, specifics-first | First-person plural. Narrative ("we tried X, it broke"). Code-adjacent language is fine and expected. |
| Product page | Confident, concrete, benefit-led | Shorter sentences. Lead with the job to be done, follow with how we do it. Minimal adjectives. |
| Press release / PR | Evidence-based, measured | Calm authority. Quote a customer or a number. Avoid hype language even when announcing something genuinely new. |
| Sales collateral | Outcomes-led, enterprise-aware | Keep the enterprise stakeholder in view: reliability, scale, compliance, portability. |
| Social (LinkedIn, X) | Direct, specific, often provocative in a substantive way | Open with a claim or a number. Never with "Excited to share." Reward the scroll. |
| Conference talk / podcast | Conversational practitioner | More first-person stories, more room for humor, same underlying substance. |
| Documentation | Clear, unadorned, instructional | Terse. Active voice. The reader is trying to get something done, get out of their way. |

## Writing habits

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with the failure mode, then the fix. Stories of things that broke earn trust. | Lead with a product feature. Readers do not care about the feature until they care about the problem. |
| Use first-person plural ("we tried," "we learned") when narrating product decisions. | Use corporate third person ("Arize empowers organizations to..."). |
| Name things precisely: tools, statuses, functions, frameworks, numbers. | Reach for vague benefit-speak: "powerful insights," "robust capabilities," "seamless experience." |
| Write sentences a busy engineer can read at a glance. Short or medium-length, active voice. | Stack clauses or bury the point. If a sentence needs a comma and a parenthetical, split it. |
| Show a code block, a diagram, or a real example early when the topic is technical. | Give abstract-only explanations when a concrete example would land faster. |
| Be honest about what is hard, unsolved, or took multiple tries. | Pretend the work was linear. False certainty is the fastest way to lose a technical reader. |
| Assume the reader is smart and in a hurry. | Over-explain basics, over-define acronyms the audience already knows, or tiptoe around technical parts. |

## Word choice

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Ship. Build. Trace. Evaluate. Debug. Iterate. Instrument. Scale. | Unlock. Empower. Leverage. Revolutionize. Supercharge. Reimagine. Transform. |
| Agent, AI engineering, evals, observability, improvement loop, session, trace. | AI solution, next-generation AI, holistic AI, end-to-end AI, AI-powered experiences. |
| Specific numbers (27M visits, 100x cost advantage, 40%+ accuracy lift). | Vague scale claims ("at massive scale," "enterprise-grade everything"). |
| "We built X because Y was failing." Cause-and-effect phrasing. | "We're excited to announce / thrilled to share / proud to unveil." Emotional PR language. |
| Plain contractions (we're, don't, can't). | Stiff formal constructions (we are, do not, cannot) in prose voice. |

## Off-brand to on-brand rewrites

| Off-brand | On-brand |
|---|---|
| Arize empowers organizations to unlock the full potential of their AI investments with next-generation observability. | Arize is how AI teams find out what their agents are actually doing in production - and how to make them better. |
| Our cutting-edge platform leverages advanced evaluation capabilities to deliver transformational outcomes for enterprise customers. | We give teams three things that have to work together: observability, evaluation, and an improvement loop. Having one or two is not enough. |
| Excited to announce that we've partnered with NVIDIA to supercharge AI workflows! | Arize AX now supports NVIDIA NIM as a native model provider. If you're running NIM-deployed models, you can connect them directly. |
| Agents are the future of enterprise AI, and companies need robust solutions to manage them effectively. | 2025 was supposed to be the year of agents. Mostly, it was not. The demos were great; the production systems were not. This post is about why, and what we are doing about it. |
| Alyx uses artificial intelligence to provide intelligent recommendations that help users improve their AI applications. | Alyx is like Cursor or Claude Code for AI engineering. You tell it what you want, and it does it: find the failure, write the eval, generate the test data, try a new prompt. |
| In today's fast-paced AI landscape, observability has never been more important. | Agents break in ways that look like success: a well-formed answer to the wrong question, an unnecessary tool call, a silent regression. You cannot fix what you cannot see. |
